## G. Final verdict: Inconclusive
CRISP shows some improvement in high-sensitivity clusters, but the variation-retention ratio indicates it may be systematically underestimating the true magnitude of query-dependent change rather than genuinely tracking it.

## H. Implications for CRISP
Judge directly from Section D's sensitivity-stratified table and Section C's cluster-sensitivity prevalence above: if CRISP's advantage is concentrated in high-sensitivity clusters and genuinely preserves variation magnitude (not just direction), the next step is investigating ADAPTIVE rank allocation (more rank for high-sensitivity clusters, less for low) rather than a uniform full-scale sweep -- not decided here in advance of the numbers above.

## Limitations
- Basis family fixed to linear_anchor_diff throughout (R1's own best performer) -- basis-family comparison was R1's scope, not R2's.
- Experiment A refits fully at rank=8 across the temperature grid; the full rank curve is only computed at tau=2.0 and tau=21.4592.
- 11 of 3811 clusters evaluated (Phase 1-3 scope, same as R1).
- Cluster-level bootstrap CIs account for within-cluster dependence across query pairs, but not for any dependence ACROSS clusters (e.g. shared blob structure in synthetic pilots; not applicable to real WikiText clusters, which are geometrically independent by construction).
